Rest · Bed washing

Washing pet beds in a small apartment: the cover, the dry, and the refit are the real test.

A bed is only as clean as its routine allows. With no utility sink and nowhere spare to dry anything, the question is not whether a label says washable — it is whether the cover comes off, washes, dries, and goes back on without a project.

What washable means in practice

Our size and fit guide starts from how the pet sleeps. This guide starts on wash day — in a small apartment the bed shares your air and collects hair and odour where you will notice both. A cover that is hard to remove quietly stops being washed, whatever the listing promised.

Judge washability as four jobs. Cover off: does it strip without fighting the filling? Wash: does it go in a normal machine alone, no utility sink required? Dry: can it dry on a shower rail, a door, or a chair back within a day? Refit: does it go back on with the shape intact?

Then hold the honest line for both picks here. Both have removable, machine-washable covers. Neither foam or insert is washable. Wash the cover only, spot-clean the insert, and never soak the insert to chase a smell: a soak-through can ruin foam, and wet foam in a small home is a worse problem than the one you started with.

The Donut wash routine

Start with the size. We name the Medium, 30 in specifically: Medium and larger carry the removable, washable cover this guide requires, and the Small is whole-bed washable only — a harder routine with nowhere to dry a whole bed. Do not buy the Small expecting the cover routine below.

On wash day: shake the cover first — hair shaken off outside, over a bin, or in the bath is hair that does not go through the machine. Strip the cover, put the insert somewhere dry and out of the walkway, and machine wash the cover on its own. Spot-clean the insert with as little water as the mark allows, and let that spot dry fully before refitting.

Refit with the shape in mind. The centre is flatter than the rim, and the shag flattens over time — a washed cover will not restore lost loft. Judge the wash by clean, not fluff, and get the nest back in its usual spot the same day.

The Brindle wash routine

The flat bed has the simpler strip. The plush cover unzips, machine washes, and is dryer safe: unzip, shake, wash, dry, zip it back over the pad. No bolster, no rim, and a flat shape that handles easily over a bath or a chair.

The limit is the same as the Donut. The shredded foam sits in stitched inner baffles so the fill does not shift — and the foam pad itself is not washable. Wash the cover only and spot-clean the foam, because a soak-through can ruin it. If liquid has gone through, lift the cover off promptly, blot and spot-clean the foam, and let it dry in moving air before refitting.

One first-week note: this bed arrives vacuum-flat and needs a day or two to expand fully. Give it that time before judging its shape — a cover zipped over a pad that is still settling will look wrong for reasons unrelated to washing.

Drying in a small apartment

Drying is where apartment routines quietly fail: no spare room, often no outdoor line, just the rooms you need that evening. Plan the dry before the wash — a cover refitted too soon traps damp against the foam.

Use the dryer only where the cover allows it — the Brindle cover is dryer safe, a real advantage with no drying space. For air drying, press water out, roll the cover in a dry towel, then hang it where air moves: a shower rail, a door, a chair back, or a rack in the bath. Spread it out; a doubled-over layer stays damp where it touches.

Timing is household reasoning, not a product claim. Wash early enough for the cover to be dry by the evening rest, allow longer in a humid week, and refit only when it feels dry throughout, seams included.

While the cover is in the wash

A wash plus a dry takes hours, and the pet will want to sleep during some of them. That gap is not a reason to buy a second bed. A folded throw in the usual spot is a valid spare surface — the same stance our size and fit guide takes to a second rest surface. A chair cushion or window pad works just as well.

Put the spare where the bed normally sits, not in a new corner. Pets follow the location more than the object: the spot beside the sofa stays the sleeping spot while the cover is away. Keep the bare insert out of service in the meantime — uncovered, it collects hair and marks directly, with no washable layer to strip.

How often to wash

There is no calendar answer worth inventing, and this guide makes no health claim about one. Treat it as household routine judgment. Wash now after an accident, or when smell is the complaint. Otherwise wash when the build-up says so: hair and odour accumulating, or a cover that looks or feels grubby.

The routine that survives is tied to something you already do — a laundry day, a sheet change. A cover that refits in one ordinary day gets washed when it needs it; a difficult cover gets washed late, then not at all.

Buy or skip

Both picks pass the same core test — a removable, machine-washable cover — and share the same limit: the foam is not washable. Choose by sleep shape first, as the donut vs flat comparison sets out, then by routine.

Buy only if

  • Your routine fits one ordinary day. Strip, machine wash, dry, and refit before the evening rest — no utility sink at any stage.
  • You accept the cover-only line. Wash the cover, spot-clean the insert, never soak the foam.
  • For the Donut, the size is Medium or larger. Only Medium, 30 in and up carries the removable, washable cover. Buy that size for this routine, and measure the curl first.
  • A spare surface already exists. A throw, a chair, or a window pad covers the wash hours, so one bed is a complete setup.

Skip if

  • You need the whole bed to be washable. Neither pick offers that: both foams stay out of the machine. A bed whose insert you plan to soak is one this guide is telling you not to buy.
  • You have no drying plan at all. No rail, no door, no rack space, and no dryer the cover allows means the routine stalls at the damp stage. Solve the dry before the purchase.
  • You are buying the Donut in Small for its cover. The Small is whole-bed washable only, with no removable cover to strip. Skip that size for this routine.
  • The real problem is an accident pattern. Repeated accidents are a litter or health question first — see below. A washable cover helps you cope; it does not fix the cause.

When it is not a gear problem

Some findings should not be shopped for at all. If odour survives a proper wash and a full dry, the source is elsewhere — the insert after a soak-through, elsewhere in the room, or a habit that keeps re-soiling the bed. Soaking the foam to chase it will not fix that and may ruin the bed.

Repeated accidents belong in the same boundary. One accident is a wash-now trigger; a pattern is a litter or health question — litter routine on one side, a vet conversation on the other. Gear does not fix health. Sort the cause first.

FAQ

Can you wash the whole pet bed, or just the cover?

Just the cover, for both picks named here. Both have removable, machine-washable covers, and neither foam or insert is washable. Wash the cover only, spot-clean the insert, and keep the insert out of the machine and out of a soaking sink, because a soak-through can ruin foam.

How do you wash a pet bed cover without a utility sink?

Take the cover off, shake the hair off it outside, over a bin, or in the bath, then machine wash the cover on its own. Without a utility sink, the bath is the rinse surface and the machine is the wash. Name the Donut in Medium, 30 in or larger, because only those sizes carry the removable, washable cover.

How do you dry a pet bed cover in a small apartment?

Use the dryer only where the cover allows it — the Brindle cover is dryer safe. Otherwise press water out, roll the cover in a dry towel, and hang it over a shower rail, a door, or a chair back where air moves. Refit only when the cover feels dry throughout, because a damp cover back on foam traps the damp in the bed.

What does a pet sleep on while the bed cover is in the wash?

A folded throw, a folded blanket, or a flat surface the pet already uses — a chair cushion or a window pad — is a valid spare bed for a wash cycle. One proper bed plus one existing surface is enough in most small apartments. Put the throw in the usual sleeping spot, so the location carries the routine while the cover dries.

How often should you wash a pet bed cover in a small apartment?

Wash it straight away after an accident or when smell is the complaint. Otherwise wash on household routine judgment: when hair and odour have built up, or when the cover looks or feels grubby. If odour is still there after a proper wash and full dry, treat that as a litter, health, or household question, not a reason to wash the foam.
