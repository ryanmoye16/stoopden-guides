Every station fits the room. The question is whether it fits the patch of floor you actually have.

Apartment pet gear fails on inches, not on quality: the bed that eats the only walkway, the litter mat with no room to be an exit run, the steps bought for a sofa they cannot reach. This helper tests the clear floor patch you measure against the eight stations our guides name — using only the footprints and limits recorded in those guides — and it withholds the buying link from anything it just told you does not fit.

Most sizing advice on pet gear is a single product’s dimensions under a photo. That answers “how big is it” and leaves the real apartment question — “what does it displace” — to the delivery day. The helper below runs the comparison the other way round: your floor first, the gear second.

Measure the patch first

Two numbers, both in inches. Width: along the wall, from the first fixed obstacle to the next — a door frame, a radiator, the sofa’s arm. Depth: out from the wall to the line your own feet need. In most apartments the depth is the number that lies: the patch looks four feet deep until you subtract the walkway you cross twenty times a day, and then it is two. Measure the patch empty, and be honest about the walkway — the helper’s “tight” verdict exists for the patches people round up.

Three more answers sharpen the verdicts. Your pet (the steps are a dog station in our guides; the scratch and litter stations are cat stations). Your dog’s weight, if you are checking the steps — they carry a real-use limit of roughly 20 lb in our steps guide. And your sofa seat height, because the steps top out at 16.5 in and a seat above that leaves the last step landing low. Leave either blank and the helper marks the steps verdict provisional instead of guessing. Your pet’s usual sleep shape — curl or sprawl — never fails a bed; it adds the shape note our bed sizing guide is built on.

Why “tight” is its own verdict

A station that fits with under 2 inches to spare fits the tape measure and fails the apartment. It cannot slide out for cleaning — and on this site, washability is a requirement, not a bonus — and nobody can step around it, so it becomes the thing you hurdle. The helper’s rule is printed here rather than buried in code: comfortable means at least 2 inches to spare on the tightest side, tight means it fits below that, and tight stations keep their buying link because the fit is real — with the warning attached, in plain sight. If the patch is a walkway, read tight as doesn’t fit.

When nothing fits

A small patch is information, not a dead end. The pattern our guides keep landing on: go vertical or go shared. A wall-corner groomer takes no floor at all (our wall-corner groomers guide is built on exactly that), a scratcher lounge placed parallel to the sofa doubles as the nap spot so one footprint does two jobs, and the smallest stations — the hunt box at 13.5 inches square — fit patches nothing else will. What does not work is shrinking the station below the pet: a bed sized under the curl or a mat too short to be an exit run gets ignored, and ignored gear is the most expensive kind. Measure first, buy once.

FAQ

How do I measure my floor patch?

Measure the clear rectangle the station would actually live on — wall to the first obstacle one way, and out from the wall to where your own feet need to pass the other way. Do not measure the whole room: a bed that fits the room but blocks the walk to the kitchen does not fit your apartment. Measure in inches, and measure the patch empty, not around the furniture you plan to move.

Why is a station marked tight if it fits?

Because a tape-measure fit is not a living fit. A station with under 2 inches to spare cannot slide out for cleaning, and nobody can step around it — in a walkway that reads as not fitting, however exact the numbers are. The helper calls that band tight and prints the warning next to the verdict instead of quietly rounding it up to a fit.

Why does a station that doesn’t fit get no buying link?

Because linking a purchase we just told you will not fit would make the verdict worthless. Stations that fit — comfortably or tight, with the tight warning shown — keep their links. Stations outside your patch, over the steps’ weight limit, or meant for the other pet keep their guide link only, so you can read the full reasoning and the alternatives.

Why are two stations checked on one dimension only?

Because our guides record only one dimension for them: the scratcher lounge’s length (about 34 inches) and the feeding tray’s width (16 inches). The helper checks the dimension that is actually on record and says so in the verdict, rather than inventing a second number to look complete. Check the other dimension against the guide before you buy.
